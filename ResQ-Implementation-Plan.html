IDP implementation plan · 2 semesters · 3 people · software only

RESQ-Green

An emergency-routing system that combines city-wide traffic forecasts with what the ambulance's camera and LiDAR can see ahead. It picks the fastest route to hospital, re-plans when something changes, and turns signals green just in time. It is shown entirely in simulation, through a web app.

Core
Best-route selection from predicted traffic, plus a just-in-time green corridor
Map
Thousand Lights / Apollo Hospitals, Greams Road, Chennai
Input 1: macro traffic
Simulated crowd-GPS "phone" probes, with Google Routes API in live-map mode
Input 2: onboard sensing
Camera and LiDAR, rendered by CARLA and co-simulated with SUMO, processed by YOLO and tracking
ML
Graph model forecasting traffic 5–30 min ahead, and a detector that predicts the next 1–3 s on camera
Demo
Web app with a live simulation view, pre-recorded replays and an accuracy and results dashboard

Physical hardware, public-road deployment, V2X standards, authentication, native mobile app

Changed in this version: the physical model is removed and the demo is fully software. CARLA now supplies realistic camera and LiDAR data, because your 8 GB GPU can run it. Roles are rebalanced for three people, the map is fixed to Thousand Lights, and the budget drops to almost nothing.

What happened to your original idea. Estimating traffic from phone GPS is still here. Inside SUMO we build our own crowd-GPS source, so we can measure how accuracy changes with app adoption, and Google plays that role in live-map mode. "Predict future frames" becomes two forecast horizons: minutes ahead for roads, seconds ahead for vehicles on camera. "Best path" becomes routing on the traffic predicted for the moment the ambulance reaches each road.

The demo that proves the idea

The hidden accident

Every design choice below exists to make this scenario work. It's the centrepiece of the final demo and the results chapter.

  1. An ambulance picks up a patient in Nungambakkam, heading for Apollo Hospitals on Greams Road. The macro forecast rates Route A fastest.
  2. An accident blocked two lanes on Route A 90 seconds ago. Crowd traffic data lags by 2–5 minutes, so the macro layer still reports the road as clear.
  3. About 120 m before the blockage, the camera (in CARLA) sees stopped vehicles across both lanes and the LiDAR confirms a queue. The fusion engine raises that road's travel time with high confidence.
  4. The routing engine switches to Route B in under a second and cancels the signal reservations on Route A.
  5. Along Route B, the corridor controller clears the queues at the next three intersections just before the ambulance arrives. Afterwards, cross traffic is served first until the side queues recover.
  6. On the web app, three "ghost" ambulances replay the same seed on one map: no system, Google-style routing only, and ResQ. The gap at the finish is the headline result.
Real-world issues

Current problems and how we fix them

Some of these come from your research, some are weaknesses in the original phone-GPS idea that examiners will ask about, and some are specific to Chennai. Each fix maps to a module in this plan.

ProblemWhy it mattersOur fix
Phones are not vehiclesNot everyone runs the app. One car can carry three phones and one MTC bus forty. Pedestrians' phones move at walking pace. Counting phones gives wrong vehicle counts.Treat phones as probes. Estimate road speed, which stays robust, rather than raw counts. Merge devices moving together into one vehicle, drop walking speeds, and scale counts by the estimated adoption rate. Test at 5%, 10% and 30% adoption.
Google already does crowd trafficAn app that only routes on crowd GPS is Google Maps with a new logo.Our novelty is what Google can't do: see the road ahead from the ambulance, forecast traffic for the time the ambulance will actually reach each road, and coordinate signals along the route.
Crowd data is minutes oldAggregated traffic describes the last few minutes, so a fresh accident is invisible to it.Onboard perception covers the next 100–150 m with data that is seconds old. The fusion engine trusts it more the closer the road is.
Onboard sensors are short-sightedA camera is useful to about 100–150 m and LiDAR to 50–100 m. Neither can see 2 km ahead.Two horizons: distant roads use the forecast, nearby roads use perception.
Sonar adds nothing hereUltrasonic sensors reach about 4 m, which is useful for parking, not traffic. CARLA doesn't simulate them either.Dropped. Camera and LiDAR carry the onboard input, with CARLA's radar available if you want a third sensor.
Ambulances don't move like trafficOther vehicles give way, so in moderate traffic an ambulance moves faster than the average. In gridlock or narrow streets it doesn't.Use an ambulance speed factor based on congestion and road width, calibrated with SUMO's bluelight device, which makes simulated cars form a rescue lane.
Mixed Indian trafficTwo-wheelers, autos and lane-less movement. Detectors trained on US/EU data miss autorickshaws.Fine-tune on the India Driving Dataset (IDD) and test on your own Chennai clips. Use SUMO's sublane model for two-wheelers and passenger-car-unit (PCU) weights when estimating queues.
Chennai map data is incompleteOpenStreetMap often misses traffic signals, turn restrictions and temporary one-ways, such as those around Metro works.Import with signal guessing turned on, then manually check every junction in the box against satellite and street imagery and local knowledge. Keep the fixes in version control.
No public Chennai traffic dataThere's no open loop-detector feed for Chennai streets, and the benchmark datasets are Californian freeways.Benchmark the model on PEMS-BAY/METR-LA against published results. Calibrate SUMO demand against Google travel-time spot checks on about 10 routes, train the demo model on simulated Chennai history, and report both.
Fixed-distance signal triggerA "switch at 300 m" rule is too early with no queue and too late with a long one.A just-in-time trigger based on how long the queue ahead takes to clear.
Route flip-floppingConstant rerouting confuses the driver and wastes signal reservations.Hysteresis: switch only if the new route saves at least 10 s and 5%. Reserve only the next 2–3 intersections and cancel old reservations immediately.
Traffic after the ambulance passesSide streets held on red build long queues.A "queue-debt" recovery controller gives extra green to the approaches that waited longest.
Unsafe signal switchingJumping to green skips amber, all-red and pedestrian clearance.A deterministic safety state machine owns every transition. ML only advises.
Google API cost and termsTraffic-aware calls are billed beyond a free monthly quota, and the terms restrict storing content or building datasets from it.Call Google only at run time, never for training data. Set a billing cap. Inside SUMO it isn't needed at all.
PrivacyAny real road footage you record shows people and number plates (DPDP Act 2023).Blur faces and plates before labelling, and keep only the labelled frames.

Study area

The Chennai map

A box of about 2.5 × 2 km centred on Apollo Hospitals, Greams Road, covering Anna Salai, Greams Road, Nungambakkam High Road, Haddows Road, Cathedral Road and the Gemini junction. Parallel arterials give the router real alternatives, and peak-hour congestion gives the corridor controller something to clear.

  • Mix in two-wheelers, autos, cars, buses and trucks for a Chennai vehicle mix
Data

The two inputs

Macro Traffic data — the whole city, minutes old

  • Simulated crowd-GPS. A chosen share of vehicles act as phones and emit latitude, longitude and speed with realistic noise (5–15 m, more in dense streets), dropouts and upload lag. The aggregator map-matches them to roads.
  • Google Routes API (live-map mode). Compare duration with staticDuration to get a congestion ratio, and request traffic-on-polyline speed bands along candidate routes. Poll only routes of interest.
  • Training history. PEMS-BAY and METR-LA as benchmarks, plus 30+ simulated days of the Thousand Lights box with varied demand and incidents.

Onboard Ambulance sensors — the next 150 m, seconds old

  • Camera (CARLA RGB, front-facing): vehicle classes, counts, lanes occupied, stopped vehicles, how far back the queue reaches.
  • LiDAR (CARLA ray-cast, e.g. 32 channels, 70 m): exact distances and queue tail, and a cross-check on the camera.
  • Virtual sensor (fast mode): reads vehicles in a 150 m cone ahead through TraCI and adds misses and noise. Its miss rate by distance is calibrated from the CARLA and YOLO results, so bulk experiments stay honest.
  • Real-world check: record 15–20 min of Chennai road video on a phone, blur it, and label about 300 frames as a local test set.

Machine learning

ML models and fusion

1 · Traffic forecaster (minutes ahead)

Given the last 60 minutes of speed on every road (twelve 5-minute steps), predict speed 5, 15 and 30 minutes ahead. Ambulance trips last 5–20 minutes, so 30 minutes is as far ahead as we need.

RungModelWhy include it
BaselineHistorical average by time of dayThe number every other model must beat
ClassicalLightGBM with lag and time featuresStrong, fast and easy to explain
Graph (final)Graph WaveNet or STGCN on the road graphCongestion spreads along connected roads, and graph models capture that
OptionalGRU / LSTM per roadInclude only if time allows, since three people is tight

Metrics: MAE, RMSE and MAPE per horizon. Target: beat the historical average by ≥15% at 15 min. Reference code is available in LibCity and PyTorch Geometric Temporal; reproduce first, then customise. Training fits comfortably on your 8 GB GPU.

2 · Perception (seconds ahead)

  • Detector: Ultralytics YOLO (small or medium, YOLO11 or newer) fine-tuned on IDD classes: car, bus, truck, motorcycle, autorickshaw, bicycle, person. Also check it on CARLA frames; if the gap between CARLA and real images hurts, add a few hundred labelled CARLA frames.
  • LiDAR: filter the ground plane, cluster points (DBSCAN), and match clusters to camera boxes for distance and queue tail.
  • Tracker: ByteTrack gives stable IDs, so we can tell when a vehicle has been stopped for more than 3 s. Several stopped vehicles in the same lanes mean a queue or blockage.
  • Future frames: a Kalman constant-velocity model (optionally a small LSTM) predicts each tracked vehicle 1–3 s ahead, e.g. "gap opening in right lane" for the cab view.
  • Targets: mAP50 ≥ 0.6 on IDD vehicle classes, reported separately on CARLA frames and on your Chennai test set. Blockage-detection precision and recall on CARLA incident runs. Report inference time in ms per frame.

3 · Fusion (the core contribution)

For each road e, combine the macro estimate tm (forecast for the time the ambulance arrives) with the perception estimate tp, weighting each by how much we trust it:

t̂e = ( tm/σm² + tp/σp² ) ÷ ( 1/σm² + 1/σp² )

σm comes from the forecaster's validation error at that horizon, increased as the data gets older. σp grows with distance from the ambulance and falls with detection confidence. Roads outside sensor range use the macro estimate alone. A blockage detected with confidence above 0.8 overrides the formula and marks the road near-closed. The combined confidence is shown on the dashboard.

Best path

Routing engine

  • Graph: the SUMO network, read with sumolib, in fast and CARLA modes, so road IDs match. In live-map mode, OpenStreetMap loaded through OSMnx.
  • Time-dependent A*: each road's cost is looked up at the predicted time the ambulance will enter it, choosing the matching forecast horizon. This is what "predicting future traffic" means for routing.
  • Cost terms: fused travel time × ambulance speed factor, plus turn penalties and expected signal delay. Signal delay is lower where the corridor controller will pre-clear, which ties routing and signals together.
  • Replanning: every 2 s and on every new perception or macro update, with hysteresis (at least 10 s and 5% saved).
  • Destination choice (optional): if a second hospital is inside the box, pick whichever has the fastest ETA.
Signals

Green corridor

The router sends a short-lived reservation (ETA and approach direction) to the next 2–3 intersections on the route. Each intersection decides when to act, and not only whether to.

Tclear,i = Tstartup + Qi/si + Ttransition + Tmargin     act when  TEV,i ≤ Tclear,i

Qi is the queue ahead in PCU, from the fusion engine, so the camera improves this too. si is the discharge rate, starting at about 0.5 vehicles/s/lane and calibrated in SUMO. Tstartup is about 2 s. Ttransition is amber plus all-red (for example 3 s + 2 s). Tmargin is 2σ of the ETA estimate.

Extend

The ambulance's direction is already green, so hold it within the maximum-green limit.

Early green

A conflicting phase is green. End it through amber and all-red, then serve the ambulance's approach.

Pre-clear

A long queue sits ahead, so start the ambulance's green early enough to empty it before arrival.

No action

The ambulance will arrive on a natural green anyway. Knowing when not to preempt is part of the design.

Safety state machine (per intersection)

NORMAL→REQUEST VALID→PRE-CLEAR→SAFE TRANSITION→EV SERVICE→EV PASSED→RECOVERY→NORMAL

Rules that hold in every state: minimum green, amber and all-red are never shortened. A reservation expires if it isn't renewed within 3 s. Preemption has a maximum duration (for example 60 s), after which a watchdog forces recovery. Two conflicting greens are never allowed. An automated test asserts zero violations of this last rule in every run.

The demo

Demo web app

One React app with four pages. Examiners see everything in a browser. Replays and results are pre-computed, so the demo works even if the simulation or GPU misbehaves on the day, and they can be hosted as a static site behind a shareable link.

Live real time

  • Streams a running fast-mode SUMO session over WebSockets (5–10 Hz)
  • Map of the Thousand Lights box, with roads shaded by fused speed and confidence
  • Current route and alternatives with ETAs; status chip for each intersection
  • Decision log with reasons: "Rerouted: blockage, 2 lanes, conf 0.91 — saves 48 s"
  • Demo controls: inject accident, cut macro feed, change adoption %, blind the camera

Replay pre-run

  • Recorded scenarios with play, pause and scrub
  • Ghost race: B0, B1 and ResQ ambulances on the same map with the same seed
  • CARLA camera video synced to the timeline, with detection boxes drawn in
  • Signal state timeline for each corridor intersection

Cab tablet-sized

  • What the ambulance driver would see: next turn, ETA, next signal status (PRE-CLEAR IN 4 s)
  • Camera feed with boxes and a "gap opening" hint
  • Data-confidence badge (macro age, perception status)

Results accuracy

  • Forecast MAE, RMSE and MAPE by horizon against the baselines
  • Perception mAP by class on IDD, CARLA and Chennai clips
  • Ambulance travel-time distributions per baseline × scenario, with confidence intervals
  • Green-on-arrival rate, cross-traffic delay, recovery time, adoption-rate curve
Results chapter

Evaluation

Research questions

  • RQ1 Does combining macro forecasts with onboard perception reduce ambulance travel time compared with macro data alone, especially during incidents and when data is stale?
  • RQ2 Does just-in-time, queue-aware preemption beat fixed-distance preemption on ambulance delay and on delay to cross traffic?
  • RQ3 How does crowd-GPS adoption (5 / 10 / 30%) affect forecast accuracy and route quality?

Scenarios × metrics

Scenarios

  • Off-peak / evening peak / saturated demand
  • Hidden accident on the predicted route
  • Macro feed stale or down
  • Camera degraded: night and rain (CARLA weather presets)
  • Adoption at 5 / 10 / 30%, GPS noise
  • Two ambulances (optional)

Metrics (targets)

  • Ambulance travel time, mean and p95 (≥20% below B0 in congested runs)
  • Green on arrival (≥90%), stops per trip
  • Route regret versus an all-knowing oracle
  • Cross-street added delay and recovery time
  • Reroute reaction time (<1 s from detection)
  • Forecast MAE by horizon, perception mAP
  • Conflicting-green violations: 0

Run each cell of the scenario matrix with at least 20 random seeds in fast mode and report the mean with a 95% confidence interval. The percentages are project targets, not guaranteed outcomes.
